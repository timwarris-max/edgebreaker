Chamfer
How it decides ▸
The selection decides. EdgeBreaker looks at what you have selected and opens ready to do the obvious thing — the banner at the top says which, and you can always change it with the Change dropdown.
  • Green — adding. You selected shapes no chamfer uses. A new chamfer is created; nothing existing is touched.
  • Blue — rebuilding. You selected shapes an existing chamfer was built from, so that chamfer is rebuilt with its own size, side and cut position already loaded. Selecting some of them is enough.
  • Blue with nothing selected. The chamfer is rebuilt from the shapes it remembers. Click a chamfer's toolpath in the Toolpaths list first to say which one; otherwise it is the newest.
  • Amber — teaching. That chamfer doesn't know which shapes it was built from (it was made before v1.5.0, or its shapes have been moved or edited). Your selection becomes its shapes.
  • Red — replacing. You picked a chamfer while holding different shapes. Its offsets and toolpath are removed and rebuilt on the new ones. This cannot be undone.
How it remembers: each chamfer stores its shapes and settings on its own toolpath, so it travels inside the job file. Save the job to keep it. Delete a chamfer's toolpath and it is forgotten — which is also how you free its number.

Chamfers made before v1.5.0 are adopted: they keep their number and their size, but not their shapes, so the first rebuild is an amber one. That rebuild also renames their layer and toolpath to the EdgeBreaker names.

Adding a bit: there is nothing to add here. Bits come from Aspire's own tool database, so any V-bit you have defined is offered when you press Choose bit, and its angle, diameter, feeds, speeds and tool number are used exactly as the database has them.
  • If Select is greyed out with a bit highlighted, that bit has no feeds and speeds for the machine named at the top of that dialog. Press Copy under “Copy Settings From”, then Apply.
  • To change a chamfer's feeds and speeds, edit the bit in the tool database — not this gadget.
Chamfer side: Auto offsets part outlines outward and holes inward — right when you select an outline together with its holes. It cannot recognize a pocket edge or a hole selected on its own: pick Inside for those. Outside forces every selected vector to chamfer on its outer side.

Start depth: how far below the top of the stock the edge sits — a pocket floor, a second level, a skimmed top. Leave it at 0 for an edge at the top, which is the usual case. It does not change the chamfer itself, only how far down the whole cut happens, so the machine reaches start + depth into the material. Each chamfer remembers its own start depth, and a new chamfer always opens at 0 — yesterday's pocket can never carry a quarter inch into today's job.

Cut position: which part of the flute does the cutting. 0% is as near the tip as is safe, 100% as near the shoulder. Every position cuts the same chamfer — all that changes is how deep the bit goes and which part of it wears. The depth under each button is what the toolpath will use.

Depth vs stock: a shallow-angle bit has to plunge a long way to reach a small chamfer, so the cut can end up deeper than the material. If it does, a red line under the positions says so and names one that fits. It counts the start depth too, so what it measures is the total reach. Cutting nearer the tip is shallower, but if the tip isn't perfectly sharp, its flat widens the chamfer slightly — cut a touch shallower to compensate. The warning never stops you; it is based on the material thickness in Job Setup, and stays quiet if that is not set.

Features too narrow to chamfer: a chamfer needs room on both sides of the edge. Where there isn't any — a thin neck, a small counter inside a letter — that vector is skipped rather than approximated, and the summary at the end says how many. The skipped ones are the vectors with no orange offset beside them.

Each chamfer owns a layer EdgeBreaker - Offset 01, 02, … and a toolpath tagged [EdgeBreaker 01], 02, …. A run clears and rebuilds only its own number. Gadget deletions cannot be undone — never keep your own work on one of those layers. To keep a chamfer toolpath for good, rename it so the tag is gone and the gadget will leave it alone forever.
