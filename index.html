The idea

It doesn't cut on your line

Aspire's own chamfer toolpaths fight you. You end up guessing at depths, and the very tip of the V-bit does the cutting — the smallest, weakest part of the cutter, and the part that leaves the worst finish.

EdgeBreaker takes a different route. It draws a new line out in the waste, offset from the one you selected, and cuts that line with Machine Vectors set to On. The side of the flute forms the chamfer. The tip and its flat run beside the wall in clear air, never dragging through your cut.

That's the whole trick, and everything else follows from it.

G, and the cut position

How far out is the interesting number

That offset has a name in the gadget: G, the standoff. It's the one number you're really choosing, and the drawing at the top of this page is it, live.

Push the cut further out into the waste and the V-bit has to go deeper before its slanted face reaches your edge. Same chamfer, deeper plunge, and a higher spot on the flute doing the work.

So you pick a position from 0% to 100% up the flute. 0% is the smallest gap — the cut sits low, just clear of the tip. 100% is the largest, up near the top of the flute. Cutting high uses fresh, wide cutting edge instead of a worn point. You pay for it in depth, and the gadget shows you that depth before you commit.

Both ends of the range are there to keep the cut off the two spots a V-bit cuts badly: the tip, and the shoulder where the flute runs out. That's also why a big chamfer on a small bit gets turned down — the chamfer eats into the top of the range, and past a certain size there's nothing safe left in between.

One window

Everything on one screen

The tool picker is in the header, the chamfer size and side are on the left, and the right-hand side draws the cut you're about to make. That drawing updates as you type, so you can see what a change does before you press anything.

The selection decides

It reads what you've selected before it opens

You shouldn't have to tell it what you're doing when it can see for itself. Before the dialog opens, EdgeBreaker looks at your selection and sets itself up to do the obvious thing. A coloured banner names it, and the OK button repeats it — so a run that would destroy something says so before you press anything.

A chamfer you picked by hand, holding different shapes Replacing Chamfer K's shapes Removes its offsets and toolpath and rebuilds on the new shapes. Cannot be undone.

You're never stuck with its guess — a dropdown in the banner picks any chamfer in the job, and the banner re-colours to match.

Each chamfer remembers the shapes it was built from and the settings that built them, stored inside the job file. Save the job and it's all still there when you open it again, on any machine.

Up to 99 per job

Different chamfers in the same job

Each chamfer gets its own bit, size and cut position, its own layer and its own toolpath. Rebuilding one leaves every other one exactly as it was.

The cutters come from your Aspire tool database — angle, diameter, feeds, speeds and tool number, used exactly as you have them. There's nothing to set up inside the gadget, and you change your numbers in one place like always.

Edges below the top

Pockets and second levels

If the edge sits down in a pocket, or on a face you've already cut away, tell it how far down the surface is and the whole cut moves down to meet it. The chamfer itself doesn't change — the bit against the edge is the same relationship wherever that edge happens to sit.

When it works, it says nothing

No dialog to dismiss

A clean run shows you nothing at all. The new toolpath in the list and the orange offset vectors on the canvas are the confirmation — you don't need a window telling you what you just typed.

You only hear from it when there's something worth acting on: a shape too narrow to chamfer at the size you asked for, a vector it skipped, a remembered shape that's gone, or a toolpath Aspire wouldn't build.

Get it

It's free

I built and tested it in Aspire 12.x. There's nothing in it that needs Aspire in particular — it draws vectors, offsets them and makes a Profile toolpath — so it should run in VCarve too, and probably in older versions. I just haven't tried. Give it a go and tell me how it went.

Download EdgeBreaker.vgadget Project page & source
  1. Double-click the file to install, then restart Aspire.
  2. Open a job and select the closed vectors you want to chamfer.
  3. Gadgets menu → EdgeBreaker. Pick your bit, type a size, choose a cut position, press OK.
  4. Not what you wanted? Run it again — your vectors are still selected, so it opens on that chamfer with the settings you used.

Windows might tell you it doesn't recognise the file. That's only because it's new and not many people have downloaded it yet — choose Keep, then open it. There's a Help button in the dialog that opens the full guide, and it works with no internet.
